# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, engineering leaders, and peer engineers/designers evaluating Fei Hu.
They arrive from a resume link, a PR, or a post, usually on desktop, and skim first.
The job to be done: decide within a minute that this person has real craft, taste, and
technical depth — then, if convinced, read the readme and browse the lab/writing.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio (fei.io) that demonstrates rather than claims: the site itself is
the proof of "frontend engineer with a designer's touch." Success = a visitor feels the
craft (interactions, typography, engineering) without a single project screenshot doing
the persuading.

## Brand Personality

Editorial, precise, playful. Quietly confident: restrained paper surfaces and poster
typography carry the identity; delight is hidden in earned moments (draggable lenses,
cut-out wordmarks, constellation pets) rather than shouted.

## Anti-references

- The template dev-portfolio: card grid of projects, hero metrics, skill-bar widgets,
  testimonial carousels.
- The cream/SaaS landing look and 2023-era eyebrow-kicker scaffolding.
- Anything that reads "theme purchased" — every surface should feel authored.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach** — the site is the portfolio piece; engineering quality
   (perf, a11y, reduced-motion) is part of the design.
2. **Typography leads** — poster-grade display moments over decoration; body copy set
   for genuine reading comfort.
3. **Earned delight** — playful details reveal themselves through interaction; nothing
   autoplays its personality at the visitor.
4. **One system, bespoke surfaces** — tokens (oklch ramp, glass recipe, motion) are the
   law; each route interprets them, never forks them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast on every surface (the surface-aware accent exists for exactly this).
`prefers-reduced-motion` honored on all entrances and ambient motion. Desktop-first
flourishes (lenses, reveals) must degrade to a complete, legible page on touch and
small screens.
